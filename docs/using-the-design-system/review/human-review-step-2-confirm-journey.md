# Confirm the journey works

**Most useful when in** service design, prototyping, usability testing

Make sure a person can complete the task successfully, even when things go wrong.

## Walk through the entire journey

Check that:

- People can easily find the starting point 
- The next steps are straightforward and easy to understand
- Information is requested at the right time in the journey
- People can review and correct information before submitting it
- It is clear when the task is complete
- People can complete, review, and correct the full journey using different devices, assistive technologies, or interaction methods

## Review recovery scenarios 

Ask:

- What happens when information is missing or incorrect?
- Can people recover from mistakes without starting over?
- What happens if someone cannot complete a step?
- Can people access human or alternative support if needed?
- Can people understand system errors?
- Can people continue using an alternative accessible path? 

## Determine the next steps

**If the journey supports the task, including recovery:**

- Document what enables a clear and complete journey for people using the service
- Identify any remaining assumptions, gaps, or situations you still need to test
- Move to the next stage of the review

**If the journey only partially supports the task:**

- Pinpoint where people might become confused, blocked, or unable to recover, and document these areas
- Revise unclear steps, address missing guidance, or clarify unsupported requirements
- Test critical gaps and recovery paths before you proceed

**If the journey does not support the task at all:**

- Pause your review process
- Re-examine the journey, requirements, and available support
- Determine whether you need to revise the flow or reconsider your overall approach

**Remember:** Completing the main path doesn’t guarantee the journey works for everyone. It means you have enough confidence to keep reviewing and testing the experience, especially for less common scenarios and potential points of failure.
