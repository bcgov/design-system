# Decide what to do next

**Most useful when in** any review checkpoint

## Deciding if you’re ready to move forward

Bring together everything you’ve learned from the previous four stages to decide if the prototype is ready for its next step. This could mean more design, research, usability testing, technical exploration, sharing with decision-makers, or handing off for implementation.

**Start by reviewing what you know**

Ask:

- What has been verified through research, testing, service evidence, or confirmed requirements?
- What has been checked by people with the right knowledge, expertise, or lived experience?
- Which findings are based only on assumptions, automated checks, or AI-generated output?

**Identify what’s still needed**

Ask:

- What needs more testing, technical validation, or specialist review?
- Which standards, requirements, barriers, or risks are unresolved?
- What extra evidence would help you be more confident in your decision?

**Figure out what needs to happen next**

Ask:

- What must change before moving forward?
- What can continue for now, if assumptions, conditions, or follow-up actions are documented?
- Who is responsible for each action or decision?
- Making your decision

**If you have enough evidence for the next step**

Do:

- Record what supports your decision
- List any remaining assumptions, conditions, or follow-ups
- Identify who is responsible for the next step

## Move ahead

**If more evidence or review is needed:**

- Identify what questions are still open and why they matter
- Complete the necessary testing, validation, or specialist review
- Return to the relevant stage of this guide before deciding

**If critical issues remain unresolved:**

- Pause moving forward
- Address unsupported assumptions, unmet requirements, barriers, or potential harms
- Reconsider the prototype or approach if issues can’t be resolved

**Remember:** Readiness depends on your next step. A prototype might be ready for more research or technical exploration, but not yet for public sharing or implementation. Always record your decision, your reasons for it, and any conditions that must be met.
